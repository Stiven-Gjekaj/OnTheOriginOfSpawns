# Adapt or go extinct

The mobs in your world evolve.
Each mob that you kill changes the next generation.

Burn many zombies, and their offspring start to resist fire.
Outrun them, and their offspring get faster.
Shoot them, and they grow plates that stop arrows.

You evolve too, but you choose how.
Select small adaptations, each with a trade-off.
Then watch the mobs adapt to counter you.
Record each species in your Beagle Notebook while natural selection and
artificial selection race each other.

---

## Natural selection

Each mob species has a gene pool.
The mod records how each mob dies: fire, arrows, melee, explosions, falls,
sunlight, critical hits, iron golems, alone or in a crowd, or before it can
reach you.
A mob that kills a player makes its own genes stronger.
When a cause of death is common, the gene that counters it becomes stronger.
Each new mob gets its genes from the pool.

- **Each biome is different.** Each species has a pool for each biome
  category: hot, cold, wet, temperate, nether, and end.
  Desert zombies can resist fire while forest zombies learn to run.
- **You can see it.** Each gene changes how the mob looks.
  Charred skin, iron plates, and bare bone legs tell you what you fight before
  the fight starts.
- **It is more than stats.** Mobs that die in the open start to attack from
  cover.
  Mobs that die alone start to move in groups.
  Creepers that die before they explode get a shorter fuse.
- **Nothing is free.** Each gene has a cost.
  A zombie that resists fire has less health.
  A mob has a maximum of 5 genes, so a species cannot have every defense.
- **Use it or lose it.** If you stop a cause of death, the gene that counters
  it fades slowly.
- **Extinction is real.** Kill a species too much in one area, and it stops
  spawning there.
  Think before you lose your only source of gunpowder.

## Families

The genes of a mob decide its family.
Each species has 5 major families of its own. Zombies have Predators,
Stalkers, and Tanks. Husks have Scorchers, Mummies, and Caravans. Drowned have
Riptides, Harpooners, and Sirens.
Each major family has 4 sub families, so each species has 20 minor families,
each with its own name.
A mob with all its genes in one family is a pure family, the strongest result.
A mob with two families in balance is a hybrid.
Each family has three tiers. A stronger family result gives a bigger buff and a
smaller debuff.
Look at a mob to see what you know about its family and its genes.

## Artificial selection

You get 30 adaptations.
Each one has a gain and a cost.

| Adaptation | Gain | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Thick hide | 10% less damage taken | 10% less speed |
| Gills | You breathe longer under water | You lose air slowly on land |
| Gecko pads | You climb walls while you sneak | More fall damage |
| Echolocation | Sneaking shows mobs through walls | Mobs hear you from farther |
| Venom glands | Your melee attacks poison | Healing potions heal less |
| Mimic scent | Zombies ignore you until you attack | Villagers and golems do not trust you |

And 24 more.

You start with 1 slot.
More slots open as your notebook fills: 2 at 20% knowledge, 3 at 40%, 4 at 60%,
and 5 at 80%.
To take an adaptation, you pay XP levels.

The mobs watch you.
If you take fire perks, the mobs evolve to ignore fire.

## The Beagle Notebook

Your notebook has a tab for each species.

- Gene frequencies for each biome.
- The families that you found. Stay near a mob for 8 seconds to see the
  name of its family. Kill it, or die to it, to learn its genes too.
- Charts of change over time (planned).
- Family trees and a phylogenetic tree (planned).
- Field sketches that change when the genes change (planned).
- Predictions, for example: "Zombies in hot biomes move toward fire immunity"
  (planned).

The more you know, the more you can adapt.

## The Royal Society

Science is better together.
Find the Naturalist in a village, or craft the Royal Society block.
Put two or more notebooks in the block to share what each player knows.
The share costs XP.

## Multiplayer

All players in a world share the same gene pools.
Each player has a different notebook and different adaptations.
Your fire build on a server forces your friends to adapt too.
